# Play affordances play in place — the modal is never a waystation

## Context and Problem Statement

For a mouse user, starting playback required two aimed steps: click the
card, wait for the detail modal, find Play, click again. The owner's
stated intent is the opposite weighting — "when I click something I
mostly want it to just play; details is the secondary action." Hover is
a free channel the pointer has and the couch inputs don't, and it went
unused (a faint ring).

Meanwhile the only one-click play path in the app, `autoplay=1`, worked
by *opening the detail modal and then firing play* — the modal as a
waystation. The hero's Play button and the continue-watching row's
(unused) `autoplay` flag rode it.

Four interaction models were mocked up and compared live: (1) a
hover-revealed play button that preserves click-opens-details; (2) whole
card plays with details demoted to a corner ⓘ; (3) the Netflix
dwell-then-expand pop-out; (4) split zones (poster plays, footer opens
details).

## Decision Outcome

**Chosen option: hover-revealed play button (1), plus one governing
rule for every play affordance in the app: playing happens in place —
directly from the surface the user is on, with no detail modal opening
before, during, or after.** The detail modal is where the user chooses
details; it is never a step on the way to playback.

* **One rule on every playable card surface** — library grid (both info
  modes), Recently Added row, Continue Watching row: hovering fades in
  a centered circular play button (~120ms, opacity only — no scale, no
  translation, no dwell delay). The button plays; any other click on
  the card opens details exactly as before. Purely additive — the rest
  state is pixel-identical and nobody's existing expectation breaks.
  A default-**on** off-switch exists (Settings → Preferences → "Play
  button on cards", the `card_play_button` entry): turning it off
  restores the modal as the only route from a card to Play. The hero's
  Play is a standing control, not the hover overlay, and is unaffected.
* **Two size tiers, one visual idea.** ~52px circle on poster-shaped
  cards, ~64px on the large continue-watching backdrop. Poster cards
  gain a soft bottom scrim on hover for glyph legibility; backdrop
  cards already carry a permanent gradient and get a light flat dim
  instead — never a doubled scrim.
* **Strictly pointer-gated.** The reveal responds to pointer hover
  only — never keyboard/gamepad focus, including programmatic
  `:focus-visible`. The button is not a nav item, adds no focus stop,
  and causes no layout or scroll change (UIDR-018 untouched).
* **The hero's Play converges** on play-in-place: it fires playback
  directly, no modal. "More info" remains the modal's front door.
* **Modal-then-play is retired, not paralleled.** The `autoplay` URL
  param, the modal's deferred-autoplay step, the hero's autoplay
  value, and the continue-watching item's `autoplay` flag are deleted
  with this change. One play path remains: resolve-and-play in place.
* **Playability decides visibility.** The button appears only on cards
  whose entity can actually play, using the library views' existing
  presence/playability vocabulary — not a UI-side guess. Collection
  shelf cards get no play button: a shelf is filing, not content
  (UIDR-025); "See all" cards likewise. Play failures surface through
  the same error idiom the modal's Play already uses.
* **One action on the hover layer.** No add-to-list, no ratings, no
  metadata furniture — that is Netflix's discovery-engine equipment,
  and this is a curated library whose richer surface (the modal) is one
  click away.

### Considered and rejected

* **Whole card plays (2) / split zones (4)** — most literal reading of
  the intent, but they invert the app-wide "card click opens details"
  idiom, make every stray click launch fullscreen mpv (plus an HDR
  mode flip on the TV path — the most expensive misclick in the app),
  split mouse-click and gamepad-SELECT into different meanings for the
  same card, and are exactly the designs that would eventually demand a
  settings toggle. (4) additionally loses its details zone in
  wall-of-posters mode.
* **Dwell pop-out (3)** — the pattern that prompted the exploration.
  Its dwell delay makes play *slower* than (1), and everything else it
  buys (metadata at a glance, action row) duplicates the modal while
  paying the most motion and machinery (portal, edge clamping,
  collapse rules) — against UIDR-012's calm.

### Consequences

* Good, because play is one click from every browse surface, and the
  count of play paths in the system goes down (one), not up (two).
* Good, because the change is invisible to the couch: keyboard and
  gamepad flows, focus, and scroll are untouched by construction.
* Good, because the affordance is a single shared component with one
  state matrix, not three hand-rolled overlays.
* Bad, because play from a card skips the modal's pre-play context
  (which version, which episode is next); the resolver's choice is
  trusted silently. Accepted — that trust is exactly what "smart play"
  exists to provide, and the modal remains available for deliberate
  choice.
* Bad, because retiring modal-then-play touches surfaces beyond the
  cards (hero, modal param handling) — a wider diff than "add a
  button." Accepted deliberately over leaving two play paths alive.
* Neutral, because touch input is out of scope; revisit if a touch
  surface ever materializes.
