# I · The acts strip — reasoning

Round 9b: a person card whose picture is a strip of their latest acts, each poster flying the pennant's flag for the act; no presence line. Drawn on G's rail card (560) and BRIEF-9's grid card (900) (scratch script, not committed). No JavaScript. Three passes at 1920, read at half size; once at 1280 and 2560.

## The strip's rule

One poster per act, **newest first, left to right**. Two acts on one title are one poster with two flags in **mast order** (love, like, dislike, reviewed, watched, listing): Cleo's Charade flies the heart over the eye. The head keeps the tile, the name and the latest act's time — the first poster's. A title with no artwork is the 6% slot, named at the foot, flying its flag (Femi's Coffee Run).

The sharing states fall out: Cleo, Bob and Femi have eyes; Nick, Sam and Ada have none and carry *Doesn't share watching* at 18/66%; You carries *How friends see you* there; Theo says *Nothing shared yet*, no strip. Ada is drawn with and without the note (1b, 3b). The rail shows three acts, the page five.

The rail's poster **had to grow**: at 72×108 a 56-wide flag is the picture and two flags cover 76 of 108. At 96×144 the left half of every poster stays clear, and the card is 204 tall against G's 194 — the presence line's 26px paid for it. The gap grew 8 → 16 to clear the bleed. The cost is the rail's height: eight cards are 1686 against G's 1184 — four cards under the fold at 1080 where G showed five and a half. Every sharer's card now has a picture — the pitch.

## The three placements and the 10-foot check

Half of the 1920 render, states 1–3:

- **(a) The mast** — 28 halves to 14 on dark glass and the eye's pupil, the bubble's tail, the thumb's stem and the bookmark's notch survive; up from down by the stem; the rose reads as love before the heart does. Survives at **28/2** (rail), 32/2 (page).
- **(b) The corner disc** — 24 in a 36 disc halves to 12 in 18: the thumb loses its stem and up from down goes; the eye and the bubble both become a ring with a dot. Fails at 36; a passing disc is 44 with a 32 glyph — a badge on a 96 poster.
- **(c) Below the poster** — 28 on ink halves cleanest: nothing behind the stroke. But the flag is off the poster — a legend row matched by column — and it costs 34px per card.

**Chosen: (a) the mast, glyph 28 at stroke 2** — the owner's pitch (the eye *on* the poster) and the house idiom, so person card and title surface fly one shape. The alternative tried, the solid heroicons: they lose at half size — the solid eye and bubble are both a white blob, the thumb a mitten — so the outline stays, at 2 instead of the house 1.5 for the couch.

## What the strip loses, and where it goes

The **episode number** (*S01E03 of Pioneer One*) and the **verb**: the eye says she watched Pioneer One, not which episode. Both live in the person's opened view and the flag's tooltip. Gone too: **"all N"** (a count) and the *Recently watched* caption — the flag captions each poster.

## The one thing I am least sure of

Whether the card still reads as presence without a sentence. *Cleo · 5m ago · a poster with an eye* says "Cleo watched Pioneer One five minutes ago" only to someone who has learned the eye; the title surface's pennant carries names and a tooltip, this has neither, and the TV has no hover. The vocabulary is learned, not read; the Feed's bands, which still say *Nick reviewed*, teach it.
