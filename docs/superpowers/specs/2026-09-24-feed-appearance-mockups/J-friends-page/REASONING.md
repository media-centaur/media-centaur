## The strip's rule

One poster per **title acted on**, newest first, left to right; every act on it is a disc on its poster, in mast order (Cleo's Charade: heart over eye). The ago is the first poster's. A title without artwork is the 6% slot, named, its disc in the corner like any poster's (Femi's Coffee Run). A binge collapses: three episodes of one show are one poster with one eye; the episode is the opened card's.

## The 10-foot check

At half, per disc: the **heart** reads as a rose disc before it reads as a heart; the **eye** is a ring with a dot, nothing else is; the **bubble** keeps its tail and two lines; the **thumb** keeps its stem and reads up from down by which corner the thumb leaves — Sam's and Ada's Carnival, the same poster with opposite thumbs, tell apart at both widths, the rail's 44/28 the tightest read on the page; the **bookmark** keeps its notch; two discs read as two. The disc is the glyph's own ground on bright and dark artwork alike: the ring keeps its edge on Big Buck Bunny and The General; on Nosferatu and Carnival the disc is darker than the poster and still reads. Every name, ago and initial, the ring (2px at half, with the lift), the opened rows and the rail's 28 glyph read. **It changed nothing**: the sizes are H's and I's, the disc's glyph K's 28/32, each already tuned at half; the disc did not need 32/36. The owner's decision did change the page: with nothing under the name, the name centres on the tile.
