## The half-size check

All five pass thumbs-up-from-down, eye-from-bubble, bookmark: the disc failed in 9b on size (24 in 36), not form. The other criteria separate them.

1. **Band** — every glyph reads on one dark ground and two acts sit side by side at no cost in height; but the band lies on most posters' title lettering (CHARADE, BUNNY, DEAD, SINTEL), and five posters become five dark bars — a footer, not a mark.
2. **Corner** — the crispest, the glyph on flat ink with nothing behind it; two acts stack in a 44×76 slot; one act removes 14% of the poster, two 24%; on bright posters a die-cut, the picture shaped around the act; on dark posters (Cosmos, Carnival, Sintel) the cut vanishes and the glyph floats, still legible.
3. **Stamp** — legible, the rose square reading as love before the heart does; two stamps are a column over the right third and the title (CHARADE half gone), 14% then 29%; and a rounded ink square is the app-icon shape — a badge.
4. **Under** — the cleanest stroke and all of the artwork, two acts side by side; but the glyph is off the picture: a legend row, at +36/+40 per card.
5. **Mast** — reads at 28/2 as 9b found; two acts are a 56×76 tab stack, 30% of the rail poster — the chrome the owner named, confirmed. One flag alone still reads as a flag.

## The pick: the clipped corner

As legible as *under* for the same reason — nothing behind the glyph — and it stays on the poster. It is the one form that reads as a mark on the picture rather than a layer stuck to it: no backing, no tint, no bar; the poster is cut and the card's ground shows through, so the glyph is the card's mark on the person's poster. One act costs 14% from the corner posters use least, never the title zone.

**Two acts.** The notch grows down (44×76 rail, 56×92 page), the second glyph under the first in mast order: heart over eye on Charade, bubble over eye on Pioneer One. The alternative tried — the second glyph beside the first in an 80×44 notch — removes the poster's top edge and Charade reads as a cropped image; rejected. The slot is the pick's weakest render, on the exceptional poster; the band, which handles two acts best, pays on every poster.

**Runner-up: the footer band.** If the corner is rejected — the bite, or the vanishing cut — the band: two acts free, the picture untouched, legibility uniform; its costs are paid on every poster. Not the mast: its two-act stack opened this round and the render does not argue it away.

## The one thing I am least sure of

The vanishing cut. Half the fixture's posters are dark at the top-right, and there the pick renders as a bare glyph on imagery — legible, but not the die-cut the bright posters show. One form in the code, two in the eye; whether the floating glyph reads as the same mark, or as a hole, the render cannot settle.
