## At half size

Filled vs not is a glance. Ring vs plain is a look: the 2px/70 ring becomes a 1px bright edge (Pioneer One's eye, Nick's thumb and heart); tier 1's ring is invisible. Plain / outlined / filled survives, the middle step weakest.

## Rose vs gold

Gold is brighter, the ink glyph clearer on it. Rose has a meaning: Cleo's rose thumb on Cosmos Laundromat reads as love first, like second — colour claims the act before shape is read. Gold reads as rank, which a grade is; its cost is a second warm hue in a palette that has one. If the grade ships, gold.

## The mixed capsule

Charade, heart 3+ over eye 1: a coloured disc on the upper end of a black capsule, its lower arc cutting into the eye's zone. It reads "the heart is graded, the eye is not"; at half size a coloured disc with a black tab, the eye intact. A tier-2 ring inside a capsule is not drawn — it would cross 6px into the neighbour's zone.

## The band

Nick's. The disc sits under his name and tile beside his inline thumb; a second thumb in colour reads as emphasis on his verdict, not a count. One author, so intensity attaches to him; nothing on the band says three. The rail hints at the title reading by repeating a disc across cards; a band has no such cue, and says one fact twice.

## Against the grade

The rose heart means love everywhere else — the mast's pennant, the l1 glyph — and a white heart beside a rose one reads as two acts, not two counts. The thresholds do not survive the roster: on three friends 3+ is unanimity, on thirty it is background, so the tiers scale per user and the colour means something different on each machine. And it is a third variable on a 44 mark: shape says what, the slot says which, and colour — the loudest channel at 22px — carries the least personal fact, demoting the act the disc exists for to the second read. The count already has a title-fact home: the Watchlist mast.
