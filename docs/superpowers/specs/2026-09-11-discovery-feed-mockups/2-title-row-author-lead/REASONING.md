# 2 · Title row with an author lead

**Style.** Today's title row (`.row` / `.thumb` / `.id` / `.mast` from base.css) unchanged in silhouette. Each row is one act. The person and time are promoted to a lead line above the title, in sentence voice at the 70% tone with the name in medium weight: "Cleo wants to watch · 12m ago", "Nick recommended ♥ · 2h ago". The sentiment is a small glyph after the verb (♥ in the rose love hue, 👍 for like, nothing for a listing). Title line second: name, type · year, quiet markers. Note third; overview in its place when there is no note. Poster stays the visual anchor. The mast still flies every friend's act on the title, so an entry carries its own "Cleo agrees" signal. No avatars, no chips, no per-person colour, no day dividers. Hover is the existing surface lift; "Ignore" is quiet text at the end of the lead line, revealed with the row (entry 4).

**Decisions.**

* *Lead above the title, not beside it.* Today's row carries "Alice, Cleo · 3d ago" as meta on the title line. Putting the person first and on his own line is the minimum change that makes the row read as "someone did something" before it reads as "a title". It costs one 12px line; the three lines (17 + 20 + 18 px) still sit inside the 72px poster.
* *The name is named once.* Because the lead already says who, the note drops its `who` prefix. The note becomes the person's words, unattributed, directly under the act — the same shape as a quote under a byline.
* *Sentiment as a glyph, not a word.* "Nick recommended ♥" is shorter than "Nick loved" and keeps one verb per act kind (recommended / wants to watch), which is the brief's vocabulary. The glyph reuses the pennant's glyph so the lead and the mast share one symbol set.
* *Listings show the title overview.* A listing has no note, so the row idiom (overview when no note) applies. It keeps every row at three lines, which is what makes the feed scan as a rhythm rather than a mix of tall and short cards.
* *Mast follows the title, not the actor.* Entries 5 and 8 fly nothing: Sam and Bob are the only friends who acted on those titles and the act is already the lead. The mast appears when a second friend has acted (entries 1, 4, 6, 7), which is exactly when it says something the lead does not.
* *Ignore at the end of the lead, inline.* It follows the time with the same gap as the rest of the sentence rather than being pushed to the right edge, where it would land next to the mast and compete with it. It is the only other click target on the row.
* *Show older* is the incoming History ledger's centred ghost button, unchanged.

**Requirements.** Eight entries in the brief's order and data. Person and time on every entry in sentence voice with the app's relative-time formats. Two kinds of act with the brief's two verbs. Sample Show and Show B appear twice under different people; Movie A appears twice under the same person minutes apart (entries 2 and 3, adjacent). Markers as quiet 55% text after the meta (In library, On your list, Downloading). Masts as specified (Nick 👍 on entry 1; Bob 👍 + Cleo 👍 on 4 and 7; Nick 👍 + Cleo 🔖 on 6); Movie A's mast is Nick ♥ on both of its entries because the mast is per title. Overview shown only when there is no note, one line. Entry 4 hovered with Ignore visible; all others at rest. Whole row is the click target. Dark slate, glass, system fonts, no colour beyond the rose love pennant and glyph. Ends with Show older.

**At 3 friends and at 30.** With three friends the feed is what the mockup shows: names repeat every few rows, and the lead reads as a running account of what Nick, Cleo and Bob are doing. Same-title repeats (entries 1/6, 4/7) are visible as such because the poster repeats. With thirty friends the lead line does more work than the poster: the name is the only thing that distinguishes "Sam wants to watch" from "Priya wants to watch", and with no avatar the eye has to read the name rather than recognise it. Rows stay the same height, so a window of thirty acts is thirty identical 96px cards — scannable, but with nothing to help the reader find *their* friends among them. The mast also grows: a popular title with six friends' acts stacks six pennants at 22px + 4px gap, 152px tall against a 72px poster, and the row grows to fit it. That is the mast's existing behaviour on the detail surfaces, but on a feed it makes popular titles physically bigger than quiet ones — arguably a fair signal, arguably noise. This direction has no answer for that beyond capping the mast ("Bob, Cleo +4") when it comes up.

**Long notes.** The note is clamped to two lines with an ellipsis (`-webkit-line-clamp: 2`). A two-line note makes the text block ~75px, three pixels taller than the poster, so the row grows by a few pixels and the poster and mast re-centre. The full note is a click away in the title modal, which the whole row already opens. Nothing on the row expands in place; the feed keeps its rhythm. A one-word note ("Fine.") sits as a short third line and looks intentional rather than empty.

**Trade-offs.**

* Gains: smallest possible change to a row the Watchlist tab already shares — one added line, one dropped prefix. The poster stays the anchor and the cinematic vocabulary (poster, pennants, quiet markers) is untouched. No new component, no new nav structure, no new colour.
* Costs: the same-person-same-title case (entries 2 and 3) shows two identical posters stacked with the same pennant; the lead line is the only thing that differs. It is honest — Nick did two things — but it looks like a duplicate until the lead is read. Merging the two into one entry was ruled out by the brief.
* The 👍 glyph is an emoji and renders in the platform's colour, in the lead as well as in the pennant. That is already true of the mast today, but the lead doubles the number of yellow thumbs on the page. A monochrome icon in currentColor would fix both at once and is the first thing to change if this direction is taken.
* No avatars means no recognition at a glance. This direction bets that four to eight friends' names are read as fast as their faces. Past a dozen friends that bet is weaker (see above).
* **Can a title-first row read as a feed of acts?** Partly. The lead line makes each row *say* an act, and at three friends the page does read as "what my friends have been doing". But the row's weight is still on the poster and the 14px title: the eye lands on the artwork, then the title, and reads the 12px lead last. The result is a title list annotated with who and when, not a stream in which people are the subject. That is a fair outcome for a library app — the title is what you will act on — but it is not the Bluesky model's rendering, where the author is the first thing you see. If the feed needs to feel like people first, this direction is the floor, not the answer; it is the direction to pick if the Watchlist and the Feed must remain one row, and to reject if the feed is allowed to be its own surface.
