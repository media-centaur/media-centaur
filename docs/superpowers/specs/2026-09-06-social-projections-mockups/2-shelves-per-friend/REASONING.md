# 2 · Shelves per friend

**Style.** Borrowed from Home: each friend is a horizontal poster rail with a shelf heading, no card chrome. Roster management (keys, remove, add) is pulled out into a chip row at the top so the shelves are pure content. The heading line is the presence sentence: "Bob · watched S02E05 of Sample Show · 2h ago". Episode tags sit on the poster like a season badge. You is a shelf too, sorted by recency with everyone else rather than pinned.

**Decisions.** A rail scrolls, so the inline window is not a fixed five; "all N" becomes unnecessary until the per-friend page exists. Tracking and Recommended share one sub-line under the rail. Removing card borders makes the tab read like a browsing surface rather than an admin list, which fits "friends are content".

**Requirements.** Watched as a property of the friend: yes, the rail is the friend's shelf. Inline first: fully inline, nothing behind a disclosure. You as audit: present but not privileged; the chip row is where you are called out.

**Trade-offs.** Gains: the most cinematic, the most like the rest of the app's browsing surfaces, unbounded inline shelf. Costs: horizontal rails are the weakest d-pad idiom on this page family (Home already has this cost), a friend with two titles leaves a mostly empty rail, and separating the roster from the shelves means Remove is two visual regions away from the friend's content.
